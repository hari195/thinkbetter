<br/>

Product-Market fit is a stage during the initial days of a startup when the product and users align. <span class="inline-link">[Sam Altman](https://twitter.com/sama)</span> characterizes it as when the user start talking about your product to others on their own. <span class="inline-link">[Paul Graham's definition](http://paulgraham.com/ds.html)</span>: Product market fit is when you build something that people want.
<br/>

Startups take different time to achieve this depending on the problem it is trying to solve and the industry that it's in. Before a startup reaches product market fit, it will iterate through multiple solutions based on the feedback from different stakeholders. Shorter this feedback loop is, the <span class="inline-link">["leaner"](http://theleanstartup.com/)</span> it is. The tricky part of achieving product-market fit is that it is a 2 dimensional problem. Each time you tweak the product, that in turn also shift the market for that new solution.
<br/>

This journey until when the startup achieves product-market fit could be visualized as a 3 dimensional surface within a solution space for the problem that the startup is trying to solve. Any point on the surface is a feasible solution (simply meaning that it is valid and within physical and economic constraints), the closer this point is to the x-y plane, the closer it is to product-market fit. In this representation, a minima on the surface would be a natural point of equilibrium for a startup to reach. Startups would then need to traverse over this surface to find the minima, and the number of steps that it would take to reach will be the number of feedback loops that would be required.  One solution space can have multiple minimums, the same way one problem can be solved in many ways. There exist different sets of decisions that would lead to these minimums over time. These different minimum could cater to different segments of the market. An easy example is Slack and Whatsapp. At it's core, they are both instant messaging apps but they are build, marketed and sold in a completed different way where the former is aimed to be a tool for the enterprise while the latter is part of an individual's social media presence.
<br/>

Narrower the depression means fewer steps would be required to reach the minimum compared to a broader minimum. This doesn't necessarily mean the it is easier to reach product market fir if the depression is narrower. To better understand this, we need to understand what goes into each step that is traversed on the surface. 

<br/>

Each step on the surface may include the following

- Customer Feedback
- Data Harvesting and Analytics
- Research Work
- Engineering
- Financial and Legal checks

<br/>

Each step is dependent on 

- How the company is organised
- Roadmap and vision
- Economic Constraints

<br/>

## Key Takeaways

<br/>

1. The journey of a startup from ideation to product market fit can be visualised as a point on a 3d dimensional surface. A minima on this surface is an optimal solution, ie, product-market fit
2. The characteristics of the surface are indicative of the nature of the industry and the problem that the startup is trying to solve
3. There can exist multiple ways to achieve product-market fit within a solution space.
