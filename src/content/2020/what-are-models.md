<br/>

A model is a representation of a real world system. In most cases, they are a simplification of this real world. Models are used extensively within all fields to better understand any system. Ranging from cosmic events to the model of an atom. Models also help us predict the behaviour and manipulate outcomes of systems. There are 2 components to the use of models, one is the academic aspect and the other is the application aspect.

<br/>

Models can be  <span class="inline-link">[mathematical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_model#:~:text=A%20mathematical%20model%20usually%20describes,values%20or%20strings%2C%20for%20example.)</span>. If the behaviour of a system can be represented by a set of parameters and the relation between these parameters can be deduced via experiments, natural observation, logical reasoning etc.
Models can be more abstract than a set of equations. Models can be in the form of data in tables, like <span class="inline-link">[financial models](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/knowledge/modeling/types-of-financial-models/)</span> in a spreadsheet. A flow chart can be used to represent a process and interaction between stages in a process. Models can be a block diagram that breaks down a system into its constituents and shows the relation and interaction between them. These kind of models are widely used within the realms of <span class="inline-link">[systems engineering](https://www.sebokwiki.org/wiki/Guide_to_the_Systems_Engineering_Body_of_Knowledge_(SEBoK))</span>. Thought processes and intuitive perception can be expressed using mental models. Mental models are just explanations of thought processes.
In a nutshell, models can take a form of numbers, equations, diagrams or text.

<br/>

## What models are important?

<br/>

The conscious human mind often can only give attention to one thing at a time. Even if we like to think that we are good at multi-tasking and handling multiple things at once, that is not true. However, it is said that our sub-conscious mind is capable of solving complex multi-variable multi-solution problems. This capability arises from spending a lot of time with that domain and giving enough training data to train your sub-conscious mind. 

<br/>

There are many examples where experts in fields could come to a solution for a complex problem based on an initial hunch or a gut-feeling. This is the subconscious mind at play. But the subconscious mind engages at its own will. If you go to bed thinking about a problem, there is a good chance that you have figured out something by the time you wake up. "Sleeping on it" is now a common idiom and there is <span class="inline-link">[some evidence](https://www.newscientist.com/article/dn8732-sleeping-on-it-best-for-complex-decisions/#:~:text=Complex%20decisions%20are%20best%20left,could%20lead%20to%20expensive%20mistakes.)</span> that suggests that it works. This is a feat by the subconscious mind too. One way to engage the subconscious mind is to feed your conscious mind with information and knowledge about the domain and the problem that you are trying to solve. And to do this in a efficient way, we have to break down complex ideas and thoughts into a somewhat structured form. This will make it easier for the brain to chew on when we are not consciously thinking about it. 

<br/>

This makes it one of the most important reasons as to why we need more models that can explain complex events so that we can use our intelligence and build on top of it.
