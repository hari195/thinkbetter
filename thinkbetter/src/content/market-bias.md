The market seems to be reacting unsually well despite the rising numbers of COVID-19 cases. There are a combinationof biases at play and here are a few of them.

<br/>  

## Confirmation Bias  

<br/>

Relief packages were announced by various government worldwide at the onset of the pandemic. This might have caused the market to reach positively initially. But the remainder of the recovery could be attributed to a very common bias known as confirmation bias. This bias make people believe certain things to be true simply because they want it to be true. When someone see's information that reinforces their existing beliefs, they are more prone to accept it than question it. This can lead to major blunders as it can turn a blind spot and hide from you the most common of gaps in a reasoning or an idea.

<br/>

## Opportunity Cost  

<br/>
The stock market might be the best of alternative compared to other options.
Consumer spending trend has taken a hit due to the pandemic. Since eating out and travelling has mainly been put off, there is considerably less amount of spending in the past few months. This also means that market participants have more cash around. And having more of something can lead us to take more risks that we were unwilling to take otherwise. Since the 2008 financial crisis, there is an overall decreased trust in financial institutions. Especially in times like this when there is a higher chances of default. This can make the market participant be wary of the more complicated financial products altogether. Commodities are quite volatile during times like these and might not be a very easy option for most investors to allocate there money to. Relatively risk free options (like T-bills) doesn't seem that attractive either for an average investor. A lot of great companies are trading at a much lower price than its book value. This  makes investing in stocks also quite attractive. Not to forget options like Vanguard which offers a piece of the economy over a long periods of time. And investing in a dip reduces the friction to put in your money for most participants.
