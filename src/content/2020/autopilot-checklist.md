One prerequisite to be in a <span class="inline-link">[state of flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_(psychology))</span> while working is to have good clarity on the goal and get immediate feedback.  Being in such a state feels like you are on auto-pilot, you lose your perception of time and a feeling of calmness fill in you. Depending on the nature of the task, context and urgency you can convert parts of your workflow into chunks of flow-inducing work. 

<br/>

Another key aspect of being in a state of flow is that the tasks should be bordering on your existing capabilities making the task at hand challenging. Not too easy. Not too hard. Hence, tasks that fulfill the following characteristics would be ideal:

<br/>

- Repeatable on a high level but are nuanced each time
- Recur over time
- Hands-on nature
- Require attention down to the details

<br/>

Today's productivity apps like Todoist, Things all try to delight users with the satisfaction that they get when they check things off a list. In a small way, that too is immediate feedback to the user. A way for the user to see that they are progressing forward. This would make the format of a checklist more visual while doing a set of tasks.

<br/>

## Examples

<br/>

1. Colour grading: The goal is clear to the colorist, the kind of look that needs to be achieved is usually decided by a combination of color palettes and moodboards. The steps can be listed out even though all steps might not be needed for all clips. Each video clip is unique and requires you to focus on the tones and shades meticulously. Adjusting the dials to tweak exposure, saturation etc. are very hands-on.

2. Standard surgical procedures: The goal of the procedure is clear. Standard procedures can be quantified as lists. There is a factor of uniqueness as each patient is different. And of course, very hands-on and requires high levels of focus. WHO has published a <span class="inline-link">[19-point Surgical safety checklist](https://www.who.int/patientsafety/safesurgery/checklist/en/)</span> aiming to decrease errors.

3. Flight checks: Routine checks done by pilots before and after flights. Inherently very hands-on in nature. Repeatable and can be easily listed out. NASA published a <span class="inline-link">[paper](https://ti.arc.nasa.gov/m/profile/adegani/Cockpit%20Checklists.pdf)</span> that deeply looks into the concepts, design and use of cockpit checklists.

## Taking it to the next level

<br/>

There is another aspect to completing a checklist and that is how much time it would take. After doing a set of tasks a couple of times, you can probably estimate how much time you would need for doing the checklist once. Pre-scheduling blocks of time in your calendar based on your time estimate is a good way build it into your day without even thinking about it. Time boxing and scheduling can let you forget about these events and making time for it.

<br/>

## Get started

<br/>

Here are some easy places where you can try to implement this:

- Cleaning routine for your home
- Cooking a dish by following a recipe
- Pre-travel checklist
- Monthly financial checks
